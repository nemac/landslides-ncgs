Landslides in Western North Carolina

Where do landslides happen?

Landslides and debris flows usually occur in areas where they've happened before. That is why mapping where landslides have occurred in the past helps identify areas where they could happen again.

To see where landslides have been recorded, explore the Landslide Inventory of North Carolina.

How is weather connected to landslides?

Extreme weather patterns, such as periods of above-normal rainfall or drought, are associated with a changing climate and are already affecting people in western North Carolina. These extreme weather patterns are also linked to landslide events.

Heavy or prolonged rainfall is a particular concern: 5 or more inches of rain in 24 hours increases landslide risk significantly.

Why do landslides matter to my community?

Landslides pose significant risks to public health and safety, local economies, and home and business owners. Landslide mapping and other resilience planning allow emergency responders, planners, and city and county staff to be better prepared to help protect people and assets from losses associated with landslide events.

Where can I learn about past landslide events?

The Learn More page has interactive StoryMaps on historical landslides in the region and the Great Flood of 1916, plus handouts on the May 2018 Pacolet River Valley and December 2018 Howard Gap Road landslide events in Polk County.

Reducing Your Risk

How can I find out if my property is in a landslide-prone area?

Use the data and maps on this site, starting with the Landslide Inventory of North Carolina. The NCGS data can also be downloaded from NC OneMap.

Keep in mind that the maps are for general planning only. See the next question.

Can the maps tell me whether a specific site is safe to build on?

No. The maps and other products from this landslide hazard mapping program are intended for general planning purposes only and are provided on an "as is" basis. They are not a substitute for an on-the-ground site assessment by qualified individuals.

Avoid modifying slopes with a high or moderate probability of sliding without consulting a geologist.

What warning signs should I watch for?

Signs that the ground may be moving include:

  • unusual sounds
  • tilting trees
  • bulging ground surfaces
  • new cracks in walls
What can I do to reduce my landslide risk?
  • Learn whether your property is in a landslide-prone area.
  • Watch for warning signs.
  • Stay informed during periods of heavy or prolonged rainfall.
  • Avoid modifying high- or moderate-probability-of-sliding slopes without consulting a geologist.
  • Know your evacuation routes and have an emergency plan ready before severe weather arrives.
  • Contact your local emergency management office to understand county-specific landslide planning and resources.

The Reducing Landslide Risk page has more detail and downloadable guides, including the Reducing Landslide Risk brochure and a guide for homeowner safety.

Maps, Data and This Site

Who produces this site?

The North Carolina Department of Environmental Quality (NCDEQ) and the North Carolina Geological Survey (NCGS) have partnered with UNC Asheville's National Environmental Mapping and Applications Center (NEMAC) to deliver and visualize accurate, useful and current landslide hazard data, and to make it easier for various audiences to access, interact with and understand that data. See the About page for more.

Why does North Carolina map landslide hazards?

The Hurricane Recovery Act of 2005 (Section 6) directs NCDEQ to ensure that maps showing areas vulnerable to landslides are made available for the 19 counties designated in the Major Disaster Declarations for Hurricanes Frances and Ivan.

Can I download the data or use it in my own maps?

Yes. The data are available for download or for use as web services from NC OneMap, and the metadata is on the DEQ ArcGIS Server.

All electronic and hard copy products (maps, data, text, etc.) produced by this landslide hazard mapping program are public information and may be freely redistributed and copied. Please read the disclaimer before using them.

Who can I contact with questions?

For questions about landslides and landslide hazard data, contact the North Carolina Geological Survey's Asheville Regional Office at 828-296-4500 or through the NCGS contact page.

For county-specific landslide planning and emergency resources, contact your local emergency management office.
